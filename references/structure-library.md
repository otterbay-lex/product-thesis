# Structure Library

Use this as a vocabulary, not a closed taxonomy. Select only structures with a causal role; add a new tag when its definition is crisp and reusable.

## Market structures

- `fragmented supply` — many small, inconsistent providers are hard to compare or aggregate.
- `thin market` — demand and supply exist but rarely meet at the same time/place.
- `search cost` — finding a viable option takes material time, expertise, or money.
- `switching cost` — moving away from an incumbent requires data, learning, risk, or coordination.
- `standardization problem` — quality or scope cannot be reliably represented in a common format.

## Information structures

- `information asymmetry` — one actor knows quality, risk, or intent that another cannot observe.
- `unknown unknowns` — people cannot ask for what they do not know exists or matters.
- `signal-to-noise imbalance` — relevant information is buried in abundant low-value material.
- `verification gap` — a claim matters but is expensive or impossible to validate before action.

## Behavioral structures

- `delayed reward` — benefits arrive too late to motivate present effort.
- `cognitive overload` — decisions exceed available attention or working memory.
- `intention-action gap` — stated goals repeatedly fail to become behavior.
- `habit inertia` — a familiar but inferior workflow persists because change has activation cost.

## Coordination structures

- `coordination cost` — value requires multiple actors to align actions, timing, or information.
- `handoff loss` — context or accountability degrades between steps or owners.
- `collective-action problem` — everyone benefits from contribution but each actor can free-ride.
- `principal-agent problem` — a decision-maker's incentives diverge from the affected party's.

## Trust structures

- `trust deficit` — parties hesitate because competence, intent, or reliability is uncertain.
- `reputation portability gap` — trustworthy past behavior cannot travel to the new context.
- `commitment credibility gap` — promises are cheap and lack enforceable proof.

## Resource-allocation structures

- `underutilized asset` — usable capacity exists but cannot be discovered, accessed, or deployed.
- `matching problem` — heterogeneous needs and resources need a compatibility mechanism.
- `capacity volatility` — availability varies unpredictably, making planning costly.
- `queueing bottleneck` — scarce attention, expertise, or capacity creates costly waiting.

## Incentive structures

- `misaligned incentives` — the rewarded action does not produce the desired system outcome.
- `contribution visibility gap` — valuable work is not legible or credited.
- `risk externalization` — one party gains while another absorbs the downside.
- `short-termism` — immediate metrics systematically crowd out long-term value.
