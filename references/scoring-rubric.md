# Transfer Scoring Rubric

Score to expose judgment, not to manufacture precision. Write one short reason and the decisive evidence gap for every score. Do not average the scores into a single number: a high score in one dimension cannot cancel a fatal weakness in another.

## Structural Fit — 1–10

How strongly do the source and target share the causal structures that make the mechanism work?

- **1–3:** Surface resemblance only; the important actors, incentives, constraints, or causal drivers differ.
- **4–6:** Some important structures match, but a core driver is absent, contradicted, or unresolved.
- **7–8:** Most causal structures match; the mechanism plausibly addresses the target failure and the main difference is testable.
- **9–10:** The same core causal chain is strongly evidenced in both contexts; remaining differences are secondary and bounded.

Record separately:

- the three strongest mapped variables;
- the critical non-mapping variable;
- the earliest failure condition;
- the observation that would falsify the claimed fit.

## Transfer Distance — 1–10

How different are the source and target in surface language, users, economics, regulation, and operating context?

- **1–3:** Near transfer; adjacent category or closely related workflow.
- **4–6:** Medium transfer; visibly different domain with familiar operating logic.
- **7–10:** Far transfer; surface context is substantially different while a defensible structural mapping remains.

Distance describes where the mechanism came from; it does not measure quality. Treat a high-distance case as useful only when Structural Fit is at least 7 and the critical difference is explicit. Never raise a decision because an analogy feels surprising.

## Evidence Quality — 1–5

How well do the available sources support the factual and causal claims used in the transfer?

- **1:** Memory, unsupported assertion, or an `Unverified lead`; facts and inferences are mixed together.
- **2:** One limited or self-interested source supports some product facts, but the mechanism is mostly speculation.
- **3:** Relevant public facts are supported by identifiable sources; important inferences are labelled; major limitations are visible.
- **4:** Important claims are triangulated across suitable primary and independent or behavioural evidence; competing explanations are considered.
- **5:** The source and target causal claims have strong, directly relevant evidence, with credible counterevidence and uncertainty addressed.

Do not penalise a primary source merely for being primary; instead limit it to what it can prove. Product documentation can establish behaviour, not independent effectiveness. A company claim can establish its stated intent, not causation.

## Validation Readiness — 1–5

How ready is the critical target assumption for a bounded, interpretable pre-coding experiment?

- **1:** No falsifiable assumption or decision has been identified.
- **2:** A test idea exists, but the target sample, procedure, threshold, or disconfirming result is missing.
- **3:** The Experiment Card identifies one critical hypothesis, qualified sample, method, pre-declared pass threshold, disconfirming result, and next decision.
- **4:** Recruitment, false-positive controls, time/cost cap, and ethical or legal constraints are realistic enough to run.
- **5:** The experiment is operationally ready, its result can distinguish the preferred mechanism from a credible competing explanation, and the decision owner accepts the threshold.

Validation Readiness measures the quality of the test design, not whether the result has passed. An unrun experiment has no result and cannot justify `BUILD` by itself.

## Optional tie-breakers — 1–5

Use only after the four core dimensions have been assessed:

- **Novelty:** Does the transfer reveal a non-obvious alternative to current practice?
- **Leverage:** Could the mechanism resolve several linked frictions without adding a larger constraint?

These help choose between otherwise credible research routes. They do not repair weak fit, evidence, or validation.

## Decision gates

### Advance a transfer to an experiment

A transferred-mechanism thesis is normally ready for a bounded experiment when:

- Structural Fit is at least 7;
- Evidence Quality is at least 3;
- the critical non-mapping variable and failure boundary are explicit; and
- Validation Readiness is at least 3.

If Evidence Quality or Validation Readiness is below 3, keep the decision at `PARK` and specify the missing source, assumption, threshold, or operating condition.

### Decide after the experiment

`BUILD` is unavailable unless all of the following are true:

- Evidence Quality is at least 3;
- Validation Readiness is at least 3;
- an actual bounded experiment has run;
- its critical result meets the pass threshold declared before results were seen;
- the evidence supports the mechanism or outcome, not merely interest in the presentation; and
- no unresolved legal, ethical, safety, trust, economic, or operating constraint makes implementation irresponsible.

When those conditions are met, `BUILD` applies only to the narrow workflow tested—not to the full product vision.

Choose `PARK` when evidence is incomplete, contradictory, or not interpretable enough to decide. Choose `KILL` when the critical hypothesis or acceptable-risk boundary is disconfirmed. A low score alone is not a reason to manufacture a precise verdict; state the evidence and reasoning.
