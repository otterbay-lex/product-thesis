# Product Judgment Brief

Use this as a writing order, not a form. Write in the user's language. Keep the first answer readable without the Product Thesis vocabulary, and omit any heading that would only repeat the paragraph beneath it.

Aim for 800–1500 Chinese characters or 400–700 English words in an ordinary first answer, with at most three short headings. Do not pad a shorter sufficient answer. Explain one mechanism; keep the rest for requested expansion. Studying a named product does not by itself mean writing a full dossier for the reader.

## Opening judgment

Begin with two to four sentences in ordinary language:

- what you currently think;
- whether the direction deserves another small investment of time or evidence;
- what remains uncertain.

Explain the judgment before adding `YES`, `PARK`, or `NO`. Do not open with a score, taxonomy, table, or disclaimer.

## The case for it

Give the strongest concrete reason to continue. If a cross-domain mechanism matters, explain it as a short causal story:

> In the source context, `[starting condition]` was changed by `[mechanism]`, which led to `[behaviour or outcome]`. It may help here because `[mapped variable]` is similar.

Do not describe a copied feature as the mechanism. Add a direct source link beside any external factual claim.

## The case against it

State the most serious reason the thesis may be wrong. Prefer one decisive competing explanation, non-mapping variable, or failure condition over a long risk list.

Say what evidence would reveal that this objection is true.

## What to do next

Recommend one action. Make it runnable:

- who to involve;
- what to do without building the full product;
- what behaviour or result to observe;
- what result supports continuing, parking, or stopping.

If no experiment has run, describe the action state as `NOT YET ELIGIBLE`, not `BUILD`, `PARK`, or `KILL`. Those action decisions require observed results.

Keep that internal label out of ordinary prose: “还没有实验依据，暂不进入开发” is enough. For reported results, use the original threshold and observation window, explain any missing evidence, and restrict a pass to the tested workflow. Proposed numbers are experiment-design choices, not proven industry benchmarks.

## Offer the research trail

Close by offering, in one sentence, to expand the evidence ledger, source-product dossier, cross-domain cases, analogy stress test, scores, or complete Product Thesis Workspace. Do not append them automatically.

## Tone check

Before sending, ask:

- Could a reader unfamiliar with product strategy understand the judgment on the first read?
- Does the reader know what to do next?
- Are technical labels hidden or explained only where they help?
- Is the uncertainty honest without overwhelming the recommendation?
